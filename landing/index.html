EX 01 Marketing · Reels script
Rough prompt
I need a reels script for my online yoga classes trying to get more sign-ups
What PerfectPrompt asks
Who's the audience?
→ Busy professionals

What's the wedge?
→ It's affordable, the teaching style

Vibe of the video?
→ Personal and honest
Enhanced prompt
Write three variant scripts for a 20 to 30 second Instagram Reel promoting online yoga classes aimed at busy professionals. The hook of the offer is that each class is only 15 minutes, short enough to fit into a real workday. The vibe should be personal and honest, like a real teacher speaking to camera, not a polished marketing read. For each variant: write the hook (the first 3 seconds, spoken + what's on screen), the middle (one specific, real benefit, not "transform your life" filler), and the closing CTA (one specific action, like "link in bio" or "DM me 'yoga'"). Each variant should take a different angle: variant A leads with the time problem (no time for studios), variant B leads with how short the class is, variant C leads with how someone feels after one 15-minute session. Keep each script under 60 spoken words. Don't make medical or weight-loss claims. Don't use generic wellness phrases like "find your center" or "your journey."
EX 02 Writing · honest feedback
Rough prompt
I need to give feedback to a teammate their work has been slipping lately don't want to make it awkward
What PerfectPrompt asks
Your relationship?
→ Peer

What's actually slipping?
→ Communication, responsiveness

What outcome do you want?
→ An open conversation
Enhanced prompt
Write a short message to a peer teammate to start a conversation about their communication slipping lately: slow replies, missed check-ins, unclear on what they're working on. We're peers, not manager and report, so the tone should be a colleague reaching out, not a performance review. The goal is to open the conversation, not to deliver a verdict. Lead with care, say I noticed something feels off and I wanted to check in before assuming anything. Mention one or two specific recent moments without making a list of grievances. Make space for them to share what's actually going on, work, life, anything. Offer a low-stakes way to talk (coffee, walk, async, whatever they prefer). Under 140 words. Don't lecture. Don't moralize. Don't say "I just want to make sure you're okay" in a way that sounds like a setup. Sign off with {your_name}.
EX 03 Data · sales memo
Rough prompt
I got a spreadsheet of last quarters sales boss wants takeaways by tomorrow
What PerfectPrompt asks
What's the audience?
→ Just your boss

What kind of takeaways?
→ What's working, what's not

Format?
→ Short memo
Enhanced prompt
Analyze the attached last-quarter sales spreadsheet and write a short memo for my boss highlighting what's working and what isn't. Start by inspecting the data: column names, row count, time range, any obvious data-quality issues, and flag those before drawing conclusions. Then identify the two or three clearest signals of what's working (specific products, channels, segments, or time periods with above-trend performance) and the two or three clearest signals of what isn't (where revenue, conversion, or volume is declining or underperforming). For each finding, cite the specific number from the data, don't generalize. Keep the memo under 250 words, structured as a one-line headline, three "working" bullets, three "not working" bullets, and one closing line on what to look at next. Don't invent numbers, don't extrapolate beyond what the data supports, and flag any finding where the sample size is too small to trust. If anything crucial is missing from the spreadsheet to answer the question well, say so explicitly at the top rather than guessing around it.
Questions

Things people ask before installing it.

What does PerfectPrompt do?

It turns rough prompts into precise ones, without you leaving the app you're in. Select any text, hit a hotkey, and the enhanced version is pasted back in place. Works with coding agents (Cursor, Claude Code), chat AIs (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini), and anywhere else you write prompts.

Does it work for developers?

Yes, and it's where PerfectPrompt shines. Developers add project context once (stack, design system, constraints), and PerfectPrompt uses that context to enhance prompts into agent-executable specs with acceptance criteria, file pointers, and "do not break" rules.

Does it work for non-developers?

Absolutely. For writers, marketers, designers, researchers, founders, PerfectPrompt asks 2 to 4 friendly chip questions to fill in what the AI would otherwise guess. No jargon, no technical setup. Just clearer prompts.

Why does it ask follow-up questions?

Because vague prompts produce vague outputs. Instead of guessing audience, tone, or scope and getting it wrong 30% of the time, PerfectPrompt asks the smallest number of questions that materially change the rewrite, usually 2 or 3, never more than 4. Hold Shift to skip entirely if you want a silent rewrite.

Can I add project context?

Yes. The Project Context panel stores your stack, current feature, design constraints, and business goals once. Every developer prompt after that gets enhanced with full awareness. No need to re-explain your codebase every time.

Is the enhanced prompt ready to paste into AI tools?

That's the whole point. The output is structured (task, context, constraints, acceptance criteria where they belong), placeholder-aware ({your_name}, {request_details}), and free of generic AI filler. Copy it, paste it, send it. Works in any AI tool.
